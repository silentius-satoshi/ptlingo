# NPTE Neuro Review – Neuroplasticity, Cranial Nerves, Ataxias, Spinal Syndromes & Cerebellar Localization

**Q1.** Which principle of experience-dependent neuroplasticity emphasizes that training a specific brain function can lead to its improvement?

A. Use It or Lose It
B. Use It and Improve It
C. Specificity
D. Time Matters

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** This principle highlights that engaging in activities that stimulate a specific brain function promotes its enhancement. It emphasizes the importance of actively using and training brain functions to strengthen them.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** This principle focuses on the deterioration of brain functions when they are not engaged, not their improvement.
- **C.** This principle relates to how the nature of training dictates the type of plasticity achieved, rather than improving general brain function.
- **D.** This principle is about the timing of training and how plasticity differs at various stages, not directly about improving specific brain functions.

**Q2.** A 55-year-old office worker presents to the physical therapy clinic with sudden, severe, stabbing pain on the right side of his face that is triggered by everyday activities such as chewing, talking, or brushing his teeth. The pain is excruciating but lasts only a few seconds to a minute. What condition is the patient most likely experiencing?

A. Temporomandibular Joint Disorder (TMJ)
B. Tic douloureux
C. Migraine Headache
D. Dental Abscess

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** Tic douloureux (trigeminal neuralgia) presents with sudden, severe, stabbing pain triggered by routine activities. The brief duration of the pain episodes and their association with specific triggers differentiate it from other facial pain conditions.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** TMJ typically presents with jaw pain, clicking, or discomfort during jaw movement but lacks the sudden, severe, stabbing nature of trigeminal neuralgia.
- **C.** Migraine headaches are usually throbbing and last for hours to days, unlike the brief, intense episodes seen in trigeminal neuralgia.
- **D.** A dental abscess may cause facial pain, but it is not typically triggered by specific activities and does not manifest as sudden, severe, stabbing pain.

**Q3.** A 45-year-old patient presents with progressive ataxia, neuropathy, and restless leg syndrome. The physical therapist notes signs of pyramidal involvement. Based on the clinical findings, what is the most probable diagnosis, and what approach to treatment is most appropriate in this case?

A. Parkinson's Disease - Initiate dopaminergic medication
B. Spinocerebellar Ataxia - Provide supportive treatment; address specific symptoms
C. Multiple Sclerosis - Prescribe immunosuppressive therapy
D. Guillain-Barré Syndrome - Administer intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** Spinocerebellar Ataxias are autosomal dominant ataxias characterized by progressive ataxia, neuropathy, pyramidal signs, and restless leg syndrome. There is no cure for SCAs, and treatment is typically supportive, focusing on addressing specific symptoms and providing necessary interventions.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Parkinson's Disease primarily presents with resting tremors, bradykinesia, and rigidity, not progressive ataxia. The clinical presentation in the scenario aligns more with spinocerebellar ataxia.
- **C.** Multiple Sclerosis is characterized by demyelination and presents with a range of neurological symptoms, but it is not typically associated with progressive ataxia and pyramidal signs as seen in spinocerebellar ataxia.
- **D.** Guillain-Barré Syndrome is an acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy, usually following an infection. The chronic progressive ataxia observed in the scenario is not typical for Guillain-Barré Syndrome.

**Q4.** You are treating a patient in the outpatient setting for back pain and diagnose that they have Cauda Equina Syndrome. Which presentations match those of Cauda Equina Syndrome?

A. L1-L2, LMN, Hyporeflexia, Bowel / Bladder Dysfunction, Severe Radicular Pain
B. L2-Sacrum, UMN, Hyperreflexia, Bowel / Bladder Dysfunction, Symmetrical Motor Weakness
C. L2-Sacrum, LMN, Hyporeflexia, Bowel / Bladder Dysfunction, Severe Radicular Pain
D. L1-L2, UMN, Hyperreflexia, Bowel / Bladder Dysfunction, Symmetrical Motor Weakness

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** CES typically involves the nerve roots from L2 to Sacrum and presents with LMN signs such as hyporeflexia. The presence of bowel/bladder dysfunction and severe radicular pain further supports this diagnosis.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Cauda Equina Syndrome typically involves the nerve roots below the level of L2. The L1-L2 level is more indicative of Conus Medullaris Syndrome.
- **B.** CES is characterized by lower motor neuron (LMN) signs due to the involvement of the nerve roots. Upper motor neuron (UMN) signs and hyperreflexia are more indicative of conditions involving the spinal cord itself.
- **D.** This presentation is more indicative of Conus Medullaris Syndrome which typically involves the conus medullaris at the level of L1-L2 and presents with UMN signs such as hyperreflexia.

**Q5.** A 42-year-old woman presents to your clinic with complaints of low back pain that radiates down her legs. She reports weakness in her legs, especially when walking or standing for extended periods. During the examination, you observe hyperreflexia and spasticity in her lower extremities. She also mentions difficulty with coordination and balance. Considering her symptoms, which of the following neurological signs may be indicative of cervical myelopathy contributing to her low back pain?

A. Absence of reflexes in the lower extremities
B. Presence of Hoffman sign
C. Negative Babinski sign
D. Decreased muscle tone in the lower extremities

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** The Hoffman sign is a neurological test where involuntary flexion of the thumb and index finger occurs when the middle finger is flicked. A positive Hoffman sign suggests an upper motor neuron lesion, which can be indicative of cervical myelopathy.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** While cervical myelopathy can cause hyperreflexia, it does not typically lead to the absence of reflexes. This is not a common finding.
- **C.** The Babinski sign is assessed by stimulating the sole of the foot, and an upward movement of the big toe is considered positive. Cervical myelopathy can present with positive Babinski sign due to upper motor neuron involvement.
- **D.** Cervical myelopathy often leads to spasticity and increased muscle tone rather than decreased tone.

**Q6.** A 66-year-old male is undergoing physical therapy for left-sided hemiparesis after a stroke affecting the corticospinal tract. The therapist notes increased resistance to passive movement and a positive Babinski sign. Which of the following best explains the cause of muscle spasticity in this patient?

A. Decreased gamma-motoneuron activity leading to reduced muscle spindle sensitivity
B. Loss of inhibitory input from the cerebral cortex to spinal cord alpha-motoneurons
C. Increased acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction
D. Increased activity of the Golgi tendon organs reducing muscle inhibition

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** Corticospinal damage removes inhibitory control, leading to uncontrolled alpha-motoneuron activity and spasticity.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Gamma-motoneuron activity increases, enhancing muscle spindle sensitivity.
- **C.** ACh levels remain unchanged; spasticity is due to loss of descending inhibition, not excessive neurotransmitter release.
- **D.** Golgi tendon organs normally inhibit contraction, but spasticity results from hyperreflexia, not increased Golgi tendon activity.

**Q7.** A 72-year-old male presents to the clinic with complaints of difficulty maintaining balance while walking. His symptoms worsen when he tries to turn or change direction. When asked, he denies dizziness but says his movements feel off. Which of the following is the most likely affected structure?

A. Vestibulocerebellum (Flocculonodular lobe)
B. Spinocerebellum (Vermis and paravermal regions)
C. Cerebrocerebellum (Lateral hemispheres)
D. Basal ganglia (Substantia nigra and putamen)

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** The spinocerebellum (also called the paleocerebellum) is primarily responsible for posture, balance, and coordination of axial (trunk) and proximal limb movements. It receives proprioceptive input from the spinal cord and sends corrective signals to maintain stability during walking.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Would cause vertigo and nystagmus, which are absent here.
- **C.** Affects fine motor control, not balance/gait.
- **D.** Would lead to movement disorders like Parkinsonian gait (shuffling), not ataxia.

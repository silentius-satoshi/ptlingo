**Q1.** The medical record indicates that a patient has a deficiency of vitamin D. The patient is **MOST** at risk for developing which of the following conditions?

A. Fragile skin
B. Excessive bleeding
C. Bone decalcification
D. Poor vision

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** Vitamin D is important for calcium absorption, synthesis, and transport, and bone decalcification can result from a deficiency.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Vitamin D is not primarily involved in maintaining skin integrity.
- **B.** Bleeding disorders are related to hematological disorders, not vitamin D deficiency.
- **D.** Vitamin D is not primarily involved in maintaining proper vision.

**Q2.** A patient is unable to fully extend the right knee because of a 20° knee flexion contracture. Which of the following compensations during the swing phase of the left lower extremity is expected?

A. Hiking of the hip on the left
B. Plantar flexion of the left foot
C. Lateral trunk lean to the left
D. Dropping of the pelvis on the left

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** A knee flexion contracture on the stance limb would make it more difficult to clear the opposite leg during midswing. Hip hiking on the left may be performed to attempt to "shorten" the swing leg.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** Plantar flexion of the swing leg (left) would effectively lengthen the limb, causing further difficulty in clearing the limb.
- **C.** Lateral trunk lean is seen toward the stance side, not the swing side, to reduce abduction demand.
- **D.** Dropping of the pelvis to the left would effectively lengthen the swing leg, causing further difficulty in clearing the limb.

**Q3.** During treatment with continuous ultrasound over the upper trapezius at 1 MHz frequency with 1.25 W/cm², a patient reports increased pain and a burning sensation under the ultrasound head. Which of the following courses of action is **MOST** appropriate for the physical therapist?

A. Discontinue the treatment and call the ultrasound supplier.
B. Use a different ultrasound head.
C. Change to pulsed mode.
D. Decrease intensity.

**Correct Answer:**
- **D.** Because the treatment site is soft tissue and not a bony prominence, the symptoms would not be persistent pain. Also, the 1.25 W/cm² setting is an appropriate setting. Therefore, reducing the intensity is an appropriate modification.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Treatment may be appropriately modified by decreasing the intensity and does not need to be discontinued.
- **B.** Changing to a pulsed mode would not create a thermal effect with the ultrasound treatment, which is the purpose of continuous ultrasound. Changing the intensity first would be more appropriate.
- **C.** Pulsed mode would be less therapeutic than continuous mode; therefore, decreasing the intensity with continuous mode is more appropriate.

**Q4.** In a study of changes in muscle strength, several physical therapists performed manual muscle tests on volunteer subjects. The results of the tests are **BEST** categorized as which of the following types of data?

A. Reliable data
B. Ratio data
C. Interval data
D. Ordinal data

**Correct Answer:**
- **D.** Manual muscle tests scores are considered ordinal because they label strength measures in rank order but do not provide known or equal distances between the scores.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Data from manual muscle tests should not be considered reliable unless the reliability of the test has been established.
- **B.** Manual muscle test scores do not provide known or equal distances between scores, nor do they provide an absolute 0, so cannot be considered ratio data.
- **C.** Manual muscle test scores do not provide known or equal distances between scores, so cannot be considered interval data.

**Q5.** A patient, who has many risk factors for coronary artery disease and is presently not taking any cardiac medications, is interested in beginning an exercise program at a gym to improve cardiac health. The **BEST** self-assessment of exercise intensity during the exercise sessions of this patient is:

A. change in systolic blood pressure.
B. MET (metabolic equivalent) level.
C. rating of perceived exertion.
D. respiratory rate.

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** The rating of perceived exertion scale is subjective and is a fairly accurate assessment of ventilatory threshold (Hillegass, pp. 558, 582).

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Systolic blood pressure is a useful measure of exercise intensity, but it is not easily assessed by the patient (Hillegass, p. 558).
- **B.** A MET is a metabolic equivalent of an individual's resting metabolic rate. It varies based upon individual factors and is generally too complex to be a useful self-assessment method. (Kraemer, p. 56)
- **D.** Respiratory rate is a useful measure of exercise intensity, but it is not easily assessed by the patient (Hillegass, p. 558).

**Q6.** A physical therapist is treating a patient with biofeedback to alter the overactivity of the upper trapezius during shoulder flexion. The patient has improved by 50% but continues to inappropriately recruit the upper trapezius muscle. Which of the following courses of action for biofeedback should the therapist take **NEXT**?

A. Decrease the sensitivity (gain).
B. Increase the sensitivity (gain).
C. Maintain the same sensitivity (gain).
D. Modify the sensitivity (gain) until visible motor contraction is reached.

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** The sensitivity or "gain" should be increased as the patient learns to decrease or "train out" muscle recruitment. With increased sensitivity, the patient will work "harder" to not recruit the muscle (inhibition). The patient is attempting to learn how to decrease muscle firing/motor unit activity during voluntary movement.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** The sensitivity should be set higher to be more sensitive or selective to the firing of motor fibers so the patient can respond to inappropriate motor recruitment.
- **C.** The same sensitivity would not be as effective. The patient has already learned to decrease recruitment of the upper trapezius by 50%; therefore, to continue toward the goal of "completely training out" this recruitment, the sensitivity should be increased. With increased sensitivity, the patient will work "harder" to not recruit the muscle (inhibition). The patient is attempting to learn how to decrease muscle firing/motor unit activity during voluntary movement.
- **D.** Unlike neuromuscular electrical stimulation, biofeedback does not produce a visible muscle contraction. It only helps with recruitment of motor units (contracting muscle) and displays the activity of the muscle being monitored. Furthermore, this patient should decrease, not increase, motor unit recruitment. Therefore, eliciting a visible muscle contraction would not be helpful, as this would increase the activity of the motor units.

**Q7.** During a physical therapy examination, a patient reports numbness, burning, and paresthesias in both feet. A neurological examination reveals bilateral loss of pain and temperature sensation in a stocking-and-glove distribution, diminished ankle reflexes, normal knee-jerk reflexes, and decreased strength in the feet and ankles bilaterally. The patient **MOST** likely has:

A. L₄–L₅ nerve root compression.
B. intermittent claudication.
C. peripheral vascular disease.
D. peripheral neuropathy.

**Correct Answer:**
- **D.** A stocking-and-glove distribution of sensory impairment and distal/peripheral loss of motor function are classic signs of peripheral neuropathy (p. 469).

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** L₄–L₅ nerve root compression would cause sensory impairments in a dermatomal pattern (p. 125).
- **B.** Intermittent claudication is a vascular problem that causes pain, but no sensory or motor loss (p. 244).
- **C.** Peripheral vascular disease would not cause sensory changes or altered reflexes (p. 569).

**Q8.** A physical therapist is preparing to give instructions in the use of a new standing device to the family of a 3-year-old child who has myelomeningocele. Which of the following actions should the therapist perform **FIRST**?

A. Ask the family what problems they would have with using a standing device.
B. Give the family product literature and a video about the standing device.
C. Ask the family what method would be best for them to learn to use the standing device.
D. Show the family how to place the child into the standing device.

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** A coaching approach supports the family in expanding current abilities and learning new skills. Asking the family what learning method is best for them is consistent with adult learning and is solution focused, which is the premise of this question.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** A coaching strategy is capacity building, nondirective, and solution focused, which is not achieved by focusing on asking about anticipated problems.
- **B.** Literature and video may be an appropriate learning activity, but only after asking a question such as, "Would having a video of doing the strategy be helpful?"
- **D.** Showing the family how to do something may be an appropriate learning activity, but only after they are asked a question such as, "What can I do to make it comfortable for you to try this strategy?"

**Q11.** Which of the following observations indicates an improvement in a patient with dysdiadochokinesia?

A. Decreased resting tremor
B. Increased one-foot standing balance
C. Increased typing speed
D. Decreased horizontal nystagmus

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** Dysdiadochokinesia is characterized by the inability to perform quick, alternating movements. Typing speed is dependent on the ability to place and lift the fingers quickly. (p. 220)

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** A resting tremor is most prominent at rest and may decrease or disappear with voluntary movement. Resting tremor is not a component of dysdiadochokinesia. (p. 220)
- **B.** Single limb support is not related to dysdiadochokinesia (p. 220).
- **D.** Nystagmus is the rhythmic oscillation of the eyes and is not a component of dysdiadochokinesia (p. 212).

**Q13.** A physical therapist is developing an exercise program for a patient who has upper extremity lymphedema. Which of the following exercises should the patient perform **LAST**?

A. Elbow flexion
B. Cervical rotation
C. Wrist circumduction
D. Shoulder circumduction

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** Exercises are performed from proximal to distal areas (p. 1035). Wrist circumduction is performed last because the wrist is the most distal segment (p. 1036).

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Exercises are performed from proximal to distal areas (p. 1035). Active elbow exercise is indicated but would be performed before exercises for the wrist because the elbow is a proximal segment relative to the wrist (p. 1036).
- **B.** The exercise program for lymphedema should begin with total body relaxation. Cervical rotation is an example of this initial exercise. Exercises are prescribed from proximal to distal areas. (p. 1036)
- **D.** Exercises are performed from proximal to distal areas (p. 1035). Shoulder circumduction represents proximal clearance (p. 1037).

**Q18.** Which of the following muscles **MUST** become elongated for a patient to transition from the posture shown in photograph A to the posture shown in photograph B?

A. Sternocleidomastoids
B. Upper thoracic extensors
C. Lower and middle trapezii
D. Rhomboids major and minor

> **Media item.** This question refers to a photograph or image that is not reproduced here. View `Series 3.B Answers copy/Series 3.B Section 1/Q18.png`.

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** The sternocleidomastoids are correct because the neck must extend on the thorax and the head must forwardly rotate on the neck to move from A to B. This is opposite the anatomical action of sternocleidomastoids and would require an increase in the distance between their attachments.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** The upper thoracic extensors are incorrect because moving from A to B would decrease the distance between their attachments, thereby slackening the muscles. These muscles must contract to move from A to B.
- **C.** The lower and middle trapezii are incorrect because moving from A to B would decrease the distance between their attachments, thereby slackening the muscles. These muscles may contract to move from A to B but may not contract at all since the arms are not lifted upward.
- **D.** The rhomboids major and minor are incorrect because they move the shoulder girdles into retraction and downward rotation. This would decrease the distance between their attachments, thereby slackening the muscles. These muscles must contract to move from A to B.

**Q19.** A physical therapist is setting up an exercise program for a patient who is interested in improving cardiovascular fitness. When performing a submaximal cycle ergometer test the therapist should expect a relatively constant value for:

**Correct Answer:**

**Q21.** A patient with syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH) would **MOST** likely have complications involving which of the following systems?

B. Integumentary

**Correct Answer:**

- **D.** Syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion results in fluid volume excess. Fluid loss would be more likely to result in musculoskeletal symptoms, such as weakness. (p. 211)

**Q24.** A physical therapist is examining an older adult who has a history of falls. - **D.** It is more likely that the person stops walking when talking to concentrate on the task of walking, for which talking is a distraction.

**Q219.** The **BEST** position to stretch the right iliopsoas is hip extension combined with:

A. posterior pelvic tilt and right lateral flexion of the trunk.
B. posterior pelvic tilt and left lateral flexion of the trunk.
C. anterior pelvic tilt and left lateral flexion of the trunk.
D. anterior pelvic tilt and right lateral flexion of the trunk.

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** The right iliopsoas flexes the hip and laterally flexes the trunk to the right. Thus, lengthening it requires the opposite motions, which are hip extension and left trunk lateral flexion. The iliacus attaches to the upper two-thirds of the iliac fossa. It is more stretched when the pelvis is tilted posteriorly.

**Incorrect Answers:**

- **C.** An anterior pelvic tilt would approximate the right iliopsoas.
- **D.** Right lateral flexion and anterior pelvic tilt would approximate the right iliopsoas since it flexes the pelvis.

**Q224.** The patient reported pain during their manual muscle testing. Pain is relieved with an inferior pull on the arm when the tests are repeated. Which of the following conditions is **MOST** likely present?

A. Subacromial bursitis
B. Bicipital tendinopathy
C. Internal derangement
D. Acromioclavicular joint separation

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** Subacromial bursitis is a correct answer because pain that is relieved with distraction suggests bursitis. The stem indicates trauma, which could cause bursitis (Magee, p. 579).

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** Bicipital tendinopathy is an incorrect answer because the patient has limited range of motion. Individuals who have bicipital tendinopathy have full active and passive range of motion, with pain at end range. The patient in the scenario has a limitation of motion (Magee, p. 579).
- **C.** Internal derangement is an incorrect answer because this condition is diagnosed when pain is relieved by circumduction of the shoulder with an accompanying click or clunk (Magee, p. 579).
- **D.** Acromioclavicular joint separation is an incorrect answer because this condition is relieved with support of the elbow, not distraction of the arm with resisted tests (Magee, p. 579).

**Q225.** Which of the following treatment interventions would **BEST** improve sagittal plane motion of the shoulder?

A. Glenohumeral distraction
B. Posterior glide of the humeral head
C. Inferior glide of the humeral head
D. Anteromedial glide of the humeral head

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** Posterior glide of the humeral head is the correct answer because this glide will increase flexion, which is a sagittal plane motion and limited (Kisner, p. 142).

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Glenohumeral distraction is a technique used to decrease pain (Kisner, p. 140).
- **C.** Inferior glide of the humeral head is an incorrect answer because this glide will increase abduction, which is not a sagittal plane motion (Kisner, p. 141).
- **D.** Anteromedial glide of the humeral head is an incorrect answer because although this technique improves the sagittal plane motion of extension, that motion is not limited (Kisner, p. 142). Additionally this technique improves external rotation, but external rotation is a transverse plane motion, not a sagittal plane motion (Kisner, p. 143).

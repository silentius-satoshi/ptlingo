**Q1.** A patient who sustained a right cerebrovascular accident exhibits a flaccid left arm. During muscle testing, the patient is able to shrug the left shoulder. Which of the following statements **MOST** accurately explains this response?

A. The anterior lobe of the cerebellum was affected.
B. The vagus nerve (CN X) was not affected.
C. The spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) was not affected.
D. The middle cerebral artery was affected.

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** The upper trapezius, which is involved in shrugging the shoulder, is controlled by the spinal accessory nerve (CN XI). The spinal accessory nerve (CN XI), which is supplied by the corticobulbar tract, was apparently not affected by the stroke and accounts for the patient's ability to shrug a flaccid arm.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Involvement of the cerebellum would be associated with ipsilateral incoordination.
- **B.** The upper trapezius, which is involved in shrugging the shoulder, is controlled by the spinal accessory nerve (CN XI), not the vagus nerve (CN X).
- **D.** Not enough information is given to determine the location of the cerebrovascular accident. Further testing would be required to determine the location.

**Q2.** A patient is performing a Phase I (inpatient) cardiac rehabilitation exercise session. The physical therapist should terminate low-level activity if which of the following changes occurs?

A. The diastolic blood pressure increases to 115 mm Hg.
B. The respiratory rate increases to 20 breaths/minute.
C. The systolic blood pressure increases to 175 mm Hg.
D. The heart rate increases by 18 bpm.

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** During Phase I (inpatient) cardiac rehabilitation, vital sign parameters with activity that warrant termination are: diastolic blood pressure of 110 mm Hg or greater, systolic blood pressure above 210 mm Hg or an increase greater than 20 mm Hg from resting, and a heart rate that increases beyond 20 bpm above resting.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** The normal resting respiratory rate can range from 12 to 20 breaths/minute in adults, so an increase to 20 breaths/minute with low-level activity would not be a reason to terminate the activity.
- **C.** Systolic blood pressure above 210 mm Hg is an indication to terminate exercise.
- **D.** A heart rate that increases beyond 20 bpm above resting is an indication to terminate exercise.

A. Biceps brachii
B. Brachialis
C. Supinator

**Correct Answer:**

**Incorrect Answers:**

- **C.** The supinator does not flex the elbow (p. 718).
- **D.** The brachioradialis would move the forearm to a midposition rather than fully supinating it (p. 716).

Dehydration is not suspected, and, therefore, checking skin turgor by grasping the skin on the dorsum of the hand would not be the most useful test.

- **D.** The r value is the Pearson product-moment correlation coefficient, and r² is the coefficient of determination (the percentage of variance that is shared by the two variables that are correlated) (pp. 319, 323-324).

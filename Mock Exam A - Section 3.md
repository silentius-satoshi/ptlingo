**Q1.** To minimize skin irritation during functional electrical nerve stimulation, a physical therapist should use:

A. lower intensity, larger interelectrode distance, and larger electrodes.
B. lower intensity, larger interelectrode distance, and smaller electrodes.
C. higher intensity, smaller interelectrode distance, and smaller electrodes.
D. lower intensity, smaller interelectrode distance, and larger electrodes.

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** Several things can be done to decrease the current density and the possibility of skin irritation. These include decreasing the intensity of the stimulation, increasing the interelectrode distance, and using larger electrodes.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** Larger electrodes, not smaller, should be used to minimize skin irritation.
- **C.** Higher intensity, smaller interelectrode distance, and smaller electrodes would all increase skin irritation.
- **D.** Larger, not smaller, interelectrode distance should be used to minimize irritation.

**Q2.** A patient who is transported to the physical therapy department in a wheelchair reports severe, bilateral lower extremity pain. A purple discoloration of both feet is observed. The pain is relieved when the patient's feet are raised just above the horizontal plane. These signs are **MOST** indicative of:

A. arterial insufficiency.
B. intermittent claudication.
C. venous insufficiency.
D. a psychosomatic episode.

**Correct Answer:**
- **C.** With venous insufficiency, placing the limb in a dependent position increases swelling and, therefore, possibly, pain.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** With arterial insufficiency, elevation increases ischemia and, therefore, pain.
- **B.** Intermittent claudication is a phenomenon associated with metabolic demands exceeding the capability of the vascular system to supply adequate blood flow and manifests as pain during lower extremity exercise.
- **D.** An objective sign, such as purple discoloration, rules out a psychosomatic episode.

**Q3.** Following acute knee trauma, which of the following examination findings indicates the need for referral for radiographic imaging?

A. Tenderness to palpation of the tibial tuberosity
B. Active range of motion knee flexion limited to 75°
C. Tenderness to palpation of the medial femoral condyle
D. Full weight-bearing gait tolerance limited to 20 ft (6.1 m)

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** According to the Ottawa knee rules, a patient who has post acute knee trauma should be referred for radiographs if any of the following five criteria are present: age 55 years or older, tenderness at the fibular head or patella, inability to flex the knee greater than 90°, or inability to weight-bear for four steps.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Tenderness to palpation at the fibular head or patella, not the tibial tuberosity, is an indication for radiographs.
- **C.** Tenderness at the fibular head or patella, not medial femoral condyle, is an indication for radiographs.
- **D.** Inability to take 4 steps, not walking 20 ft (6.1 m), is an indication for radiographs.

**Q4.** Treatment of a patient with hemophilia who has a subacute hemarthrosis of the knee should **INITIALLY** include:

A. active assistive range-of-motion exercise to the knee.
B. instruction of the patient for weight-bearing to tolerance.
C. gentle resistive range-of-motion exercise to the knee.
D. continuous immobilization of the knee in an extension splint.

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** In this stage of hemarthrosis, there is still some bleeding into the joint space, but it is not as extensive as during the acute phase. Therefore, the patient will benefit from range-of-motion exercise to prevent contracture. The patient may need active-assistive exercises, because there may still be pain or edema in the joint that prevents independent performance of range of motion.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** The mechanical trauma of weight-bearing to tolerance at this stage may impinge on and damage the pathologic synovium within the joint.
- **C.** Resistive range of motion is more appropriate when pain and swelling have subsided and bleeding is not occurring.
- **D.** Continuous immobilization in the extended position will promote contracture in the edematous knee.

**Q11.** While a physical therapist is performing transfer training from bed to chair with a patient who had a total knee arthroplasty 2 days ago, the electrocardiograph monitor alarms and the therapist notes that premature ventricular contractions have developed. What is the therapist's **BEST** course of action at this time?

A. Position the patient on a stable surface and discontinue physical therapy for the day.
B. Continue the transfer to the chair and monitor the patient's oxygen saturation levels.
C. Continue the transfer to the chair and immediately notify the nurse about the premature ventricular contractions.
D. Position the patient on a stable surface and determine the frequency of the premature ventricular contractions.

**Correct Answer:**
- **D.** Positioning the patient on a stable surface and determining the frequency of the premature ventricular contractions are the most appropriate actions. The therapist should monitor the electrocardiogram and blood pressure of the patient. Onset of premature ventricular contractions can be benign or stable. Less than six contractions/minute is generally stable, while greater than six contractions/minute is considered less stable. A physical therapist should be able to determine this stability.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Positioning the patient on a stable surface and discontinuing physical therapy for the day would apply only after the therapist has done further analysis of the situation and determined the frequency of premature ventricular contractions.
- **B.** Monitoring oxygen saturation is useful but not the most immediate course of action.
- **C.** Although notifying the nurse is something the therapist should do, this should be done after an initial evaluation of the frequency of the premature ventricular contractions.

- **C.** Although stretching the flexor digitorum profundus might improve DIP extension which is present in this deformity, it can also worsen the PIP extension. Therefore this is not the correct answer.
- **D.** Stretching the flexor digitorum profundus can also worsen the PIP hyperextension. Therefore this is not the correct answer.

**Q15.** Which of the following descriptions **BEST** characterizes stable angina?

A. Episodes of nonradiating chest pain each lasting 5-15 minutes
B. Episodes of severe chest pain each lasting longer than 15 minutes
C. Chest pain occurring at rest and unaffected by exertion
D. Chest pain accompanied by dysrhythmias

**Correct Answer:**
- **A.** Stable angina generally occurs during physical effort and is characterized by substernal, usually nonradiating pain lasting between 5 and 15 minutes.

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **B.** In unstable angina, the episodes occur during physical exertion or psychological stress and are more frequent, the pain may be severe, and the duration of each event is usually greater than 15 minutes.
- **C.** Variant angina occurs while the individual is at rest, usually during waking and at the same hour of the day.
- **D.** Dysrhythmias occur more commonly in individuals who have variant angina than in those with exertional angina (either stable or unstable).

**Q26.** Following a recent inguinal hernia repair, a patient has moderate lower extremity pitting edema and tightness not relieved by elevation of the leg. Which of the following guidelines would be **MOST** appropriate?

A. Avoidance of compression
B. Use of short-stretch bandages
C. Use of low compression garments
D. Use of long-stretch bandages

**Correct Answer:**
- **B.** The stem describes a patient who has lymphedema. Lymphedema has swelling distal to the area of impaired lymphatic function that is unrelieved by elevation. There is also pitting edema in the early stages of the disease. Patients have feelings of fatigue, heaviness, pressure, or tightness in the affected region. Short stretch compression wraps (bandaging) are worn 23 hours a day until limb reduction goals are reached, then moderate to high compression garments are used for maintenance of limb reduction. (Chapter 14, Compression guidelines immediately under figure 14.46)

**Incorrect Answers:**
- **A.** Low to no compression is indicated for arterial wounds. (Chapter 14, Compression guidelines immediately under figure 14.46). Arterial insufficiency would present with wounds frequently located in the lower extremities, decreased pulses, and pallor when elevated. Swelling is not a usual characteristic of arterial insufficiency. The patient description in the stem does not align with a presentation of arterial insufficiency. The stem describes a patient who has lymphedema and for whom short stretch bandages would be the most appropriate intervention.
- **C.** Low to no compression is indicated in arterial wounds. An off-the-shelf low compression garment of 12-25 mm Hg is considered low compression. The patient description in the stem does not align with a presentation of arterial insufficiency. The stem describes a patient who has lymphedema and for whom short stretch bandages would be the most appropriate intervention.
- **D.** Long stretch bandages provide high resting pressure, meaning they constrict when the wearer is resting. Because of their stretchiness, they don't provide adequate working pressure. Short stretch bandages have a low resting pressure and high working pressure, meaning they provide a more rigid shell when applied to a limb. This makes short stretch bandages more appropriate for treating edema and lymphedema. (Chapter 14, text above figure 14.39)
